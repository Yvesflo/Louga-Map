About / À propos

This platform compares three approaches to measure geographic accessibility from villages to the nearest Case de Santé (health centre), all applied within a 10 km buffer around each facility.

  1. Euclidean (straight-line) distance (QGIS Distance Matrix): the simplest and fastest measure. It ignores the road network, so it often underestimates real travel distances.
  2. Road network distance (QNEAT3): instead of a straight line, it routes each village to its nearest facility along the actual road network using Dijkstra’s shortest path. The difference vs Euclidean reflects the cost added by road geometry.
  3. LCP (Least Cost Path) (AccessMod): accounts for terrain using a Digital Elevation Model (SRTM) to model how slope and land cover affect movement. It also considers natural barriers (rivers, wetlands, relief features) that must be skirted around. This generally produces the most realistic travel effort.
How to navigate
  • Toggle techniques: use the “View / Vue” radio buttons to switch between Euclidien, Road network and LCP.
  • Filter by distance: move the “Max distance” slider to show villages whose nearest access is within the selected threshold.
  • Legend: click distance bands in the legend to show/hide them on the map.
  • Search: type a village/admin name in the search box, then click a result to fly to it.
Limitations

All three approaches depend on the quality and completeness of their input data. Results should be interpreted with that in mind.

  • Euclidean (straight-line): the results can be less meaningful where terrain is complex or where road coverage is sparse, because it assumes frictionless travel in a straight line.
  • Road network (QNEAT3): relies on the completeness of the OpenStreetMap road network. In rural areas, informal paths/tracks may be missing from OSM. Where a village cannot be connected to any road, QNEAT3 returns null—this does not necessarily mean the village is physically isolated, only that no mapped road reaches it.
  • LCP (AccessMod): sensitive to the DEM resolution (e.g., SRTM ~90 m) and the accuracy of the land cover and barrier layers used in AccessMod. A village located on a mapped barrier may return null because the algorithm cannot compute a cost surface from an impassable origin point. LCP also does not account for road quality, seasonal effects (e.g., flooding), or the actual mode of transport used.
  • Static analysis: the assessment is time-independent and does not capture temporal variation such as seasonal road degradation during the rainy season.
